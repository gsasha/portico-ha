# transport-rs parity: gate_sip_observer driver

> **RETIRED (DEV-518 C5).** The `gate_sip_observer` driver was deleted from `transport/` and `transport-rs/` after the observer door entry was deleted live on 2026-11-22. This page is kept as the history behind the stable difference numbers 82 to 97, 340 to 348, 362, 464 and 465; the code, tests and golden vectors it names no longer exist.

Scope: the `gate_sip_observer` driver: the poll driver and its listen leg (5a), the claim and the dial (5b), and its HTTP client.

The numbered differences of the whole port are indexed in [../../PARITY.md](../../PARITY.md); their numbers are stable (code comments, tests, the review log and the contract map cite them), so a new difference takes the next free number of its range and goes into the file of its subject. "Smoke" means `scripts/ipc_smoke.py` gave byte-identical output on both binaries, "unit" means a `cargo test` case. Reference: Go `transport/` at the branch point of `dev-764-rust-skeleton` (main `a52af72`), built with `golang:1.23`. Long table cells are shortened to a summary with the full text below the table.

## gate_sip_observer, the poll driver and its listen leg (slice 5a)

Reference: Go `transport/pkg/driver/gatesipobserver/{gatesipobserver,listen}.go` plus the bookkeeping of `dial.go` that rides on the poll (`parseDial`, `canDial`, `canDialNow`, `publishDial`, `markPolled`) at current `main` (`5d5f10d`). **Go drift check** (`git log --oneline a52af72..HEAD -- transport/pkg/driver/gatesipobserver transport/cmd`): one commit, `9ec8271` ("don't play the audio queued while the phone was connecting"), which touches `claim.go` and `claim_test.go` only: slice 5b's. `fe4dafe` ("a bell press during my own call out hangs it up", named in the brief) touched `custom_components/portico/door/*` and a comment in `pkg/driver/unifiprotect/talk_dial.go`: nothing in the observer or `cmd/`, so nothing for 5a. The port is of the current behaviour.

Split with slice 5b: **5a** is the driver, its configuration, the `/health` poll, ring/call-state detection, `driver_availability`, the capabilities (including the `CanDial` republish through `capabilities_changed`), the refusals of Dial/Answer/OpenRelay as `not_supported`, and the listen leg (`listen.go`: greeting, control codes, frame reading, reconnect and backoff). **5b** is `claim.go` (CLAIM, `audioLink`, `ownerWriter`, resume, hangup of a held call, `ClaimedAudio`) and the placing of a call in `dial.go` (`DialCall` after the verdict check, `dialHangup`, `dialAudio`). What 5a leaves for it, so that 5b adds code and redesigns nothing: `Session` already shares its write half (`Arc<Mutex<OwnedWriteHalf>>`) and carries `quit`, `frame_b`; `LegShared` has `set_claims_off` for the REFUSED reason; `LegTimings` has every clock of `claim.go`/`dial.go`; the run loop of `listen.rs` marks with `5b:` where `pending` (a claim waiting), `owned` (the held call) and `resuming` plug in; `GateSipObserver::{answer, dial_call, hangup_call, claimed_audio}` are the four entry points and return the typed errors below; `Run` in `mod.rs` has the place where `Stop` waits for a live dial.

### Go oddities found (ported, not fixed)

* **A `/health` whose `registered` or `lastErr` has the wrong type fails every poll**, although the driver never reads either (they are decoded into `bool` and `string` and never used). Likewise `calls`/`answered` written as `1.0` fail (`json: cannot unmarshal number 1.0 into Go struct field health.calls of type int64`). A gate-sip release that changed one of these would take the device down. The port keeps it (golden scenarios "registered is a string", "calls is fractional"). Candidate Go-side fix: decode only the fields that are read.
* `Duration` for `interval` accepts a negative or zero duration string and then refuses it as "below the minimum" (`interval -5s is below the 250ms minimum`); a JSON number above 3600 is refused as "not a usable interval" with Go's `%v` of the float (`1e+07`).
* The truncated-literal error reads `invalid character ' ' in literal true (expecting 'u')` (the scanner is fed a space at end of input): Python shows a reason that names a space nobody sent.
* On a REUSED connection that the server closed without a reply, `net/http` retries the GET once on a new connection: a gate-sip that is half-dead consumes two requests for one poll. The Rust client does the same.
* `listen.go` ignores everything but GOODBYE from a `legSession.leave` that races a failed write; `leave` is best-effort by design.
* An unrecognised `ERR` reason is classed permanent (a minute of silence) and a REFUSED/GRANTED nobody asked for is not an error to log: the leg only changes state.

### Not verified (5a)

* Against a real gate-sip: only its documented wire protocol (README "Listen mode and claims", `/health` as `gatesipobserver_test.go` renders it) was available. The live estate's gate-sip is plain http; an `https` URL is polled over TLS since R1-9 (row 82, difference 341).
* The 91 scenarios are real-time. They passed repeatedly on the build host, `golden_observer` takes about 23 s (its two halves run in parallel), and a heavily loaded machine can in principle delay a state past a wait (the waits are 8 to 20 s). A failure there that disappears when the test is run alone is load, not a mismatch.
* Behaviour under a gate-sip that sends a very large or slowly dripping response is covered by the body cap and the timeout, not by a soak.
* Panics are injected (a seam flag), never organic: the poll loop's and the leg's restarts are tested, the GOODBYE of a panicked leg is best effort (a task spawned from `Session`'s drop, 1 s limit).
* The first poll after `Start` with `dial_enabled` on can delay the `configure` reply by up to the request timeout + 250 ms (as in Go).

### Go oddities found (ported, not fixed; for DEV-770)

* **A second GRANTED on a socket that already owns the call (no resume pending) hangs the held call up**: `evSessGranted`'s default branch closes the session (GOODBYE) and sets `doneCall`, and the next pass of the loop releases the call because `lostAt` is zero ("could not resume inside gate-sip's grace"). gate-sip sends one GRANTED per CLAIM, so it needs a gate-sip bug or a duplicated frame, but the effect is a user's call ended. The port keeps it. Candidate Go fix: ignore a GRANTED while owning and not resuming.
* A caller that gives up after its CLAIM is on the wire leaves `pending` alive: a later GRANTED creates an owned call nobody waits for, which is hung up after `sink_attach` (15 s). This matches Go (found by the independent review of 5b).
* The 1 s write deadline of `writeRaw` can leave half a frame on the wire (`Write` returns after a partial write); the next frame then continues the stream and gate-sip reads garbage lengths. The Rust write is cancelled by its timeout in the same way. Both rely on the idle rule and the resume to recover.
* `HangupCall` of a dial or of a held call answers `busy` instead of waiting when a hang-up is already queued (cap 1 for a dial, 4 for the leg): a double tap on "hang up" can show an error although the first one worked.
* An event queued for the sink right before `Stop` is dropped by design ("events still queued at Stop are dropped"): a `DialCall` immediately followed by `Stop` may never report `answered`. Harmless at shutdown, and why the golden scenario waits for the event first.
* `dialHangup` answers success when the call's `done` closes first, even if the call ended for another reason and the hang-up was never executed.

### Not verified (5b)

* Against a real gate-sip, a real door station or a real browser: only the documented wire protocol (README "Listen mode and claims" and "Talk to the gate, placed by Portico") and fakes built from it. The audio is checked for length, order, rate conversion and gain, never by ear.
* The two fakes of the golden (Go's, Rust's) are written separately from the same description; a defect common to both would not show.
* Panics in the claim code are injected through the existing seam (the leg's next pass), not organic; a panic in the dial's call task is injected since Phase R round 2 (`panic_dial_for_test`, `tests/observer_dial.rs` `a_panic_in_the_call_task_ends_the_call_once_and_frees_the_slot`).
* The scenarios and the ported tests are real-time (`golden_claim` about 25 s, the claim and dial tests about 6 s and 3 s). A heavily loaded host can in principle delay a state past a wait (the waits are 6 to 8 s); a failure that disappears when the test is run alone is load.
* `TestEveryClaimSessionGoroutineEndsWithStop` is checked by its observables, not by counting tasks.

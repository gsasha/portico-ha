# transport-rs parity: wsmedia

Scope: `internal/wsmedia`: the away phone's audio over the signalling WebSocket joined to a device leg.

The numbered differences of the whole port are indexed in [../../PARITY.md](../../PARITY.md); their numbers are stable (code comments, tests, the review log and the contract map cite them), so a new difference takes the next free number of its range and goes into the file of its subject. "Smoke" means `scripts/ipc_smoke.py` gave byte-identical output on both binaries, "unit" means a `cargo test` case. Reference: Go `transport/` at the branch point of `dev-764-rust-skeleton` (main `a52af72`), built with `golang:1.23`. Long table cells are shortened to a summary with the full text below the table.

**Row 158, Why column, in full.** the hub's `media_closed` is an awaited IPC write (Go's `Notify` blocks the same way). Test `a_panicking_close_callback_does_not_stop_the_others`. Notes: `Leg::close` takes the callbacks out of the leg before running them, so a future dropped mid-callback (the serve task torn down at shutdown) loses the remaining ones, including the device hangup (Go's synchronous run cannot be dropped); and `media_closed` is awaited before the device-hangup callbacks, so a stalled IPC write delays the hangup by the write timeout (the same in Go)

### Go defects and oddities found (kept in the port; for DEV-770)

* **The transport never closes the phone's leg when the device call ends** (the VTO hangs up, `ended` is notified): the leg stays until Python sends `media_close`, and a phone that keeps sending frames keeps the idle watchdog quiet for ever, with `media_frame` still answering `{"ok":true}` while the audio goes nowhere. Python's `away_media.py` must close on `call_state_changed ended`. Kept (smoke flow: "a frame after the VTO hung up is still accepted").
* **`media_open` for a call nobody holds succeeds** and opens a leg that carries nothing (until the idle close, or for ever if the phone keeps sending); the join found nothing and nobody is told. Python decides who may open one. Kept.
* **`join` is called with the hub lock released but after the leg is in the map**: a `media_frame` for the id can arrive between the two and is accepted into a leg whose join has not run yet. Harmless (the frame waits in the 8-frame channel), and the same in Rust.
* **`wsmedia.New` is given `idle` as a duration of any size**: the hub passes the 5 s constant, but a zero would close the leg in the same instant. Not reachable from the IPC.
* Go's `Leg.Push` on an `l16` leg copies the frame and on a `pcmu` leg builds a new one: no aliasing of Python's buffer. (Not a defect; the Rust copy is the same.)
* A `call_id` or `codec` with a lone surrogate escape collides with the same text carrying U+FFFD (two different JSON ids become one). Not reachable from Python's ids.

### Smoke

`python3 scripts/ipc_smoke.py <binary> wsmedia` (also part of the full run, after `dahua_flow`): a fake VTO on 127.0.0.9 rings; the VTO is not answered before `media_open`; the 200 follows at once (SDP: L16/16000, ptime 40); a repeated `media_open` is `already_open`; the VTO's 16 kHz tone arrives as `media_frame` notifications; four phone frames arrive at the VTO as 1280-byte RTP (one md5); `media_close` prints `media_closed` then the response and the VTO gets our BYE; a frame after the close is `unknown_session`; a second ring is answered by an `l16` leg, the VTO hangs up first (`ended`), a frame is still accepted, `media_close`. The relay's jitter buffer fills gaps with mu-law/PCM silence frames at a rate that depends on timing (both binaries do it), so the script does not count those lines. Go and Rust output are identical, twice (diff empty).

### Not verified (8)

* **No real phone and no real Python.** `away_media.py` and the Home Assistant side were not in the loop: the frames come from a test client and the smoke script, so the phone's pacing (40 ms frames over a real WebSocket, bursts after a stall) is untested; the 8-frame channels drop silently under a burst, as in Go.
* **Real-time tests.** The golden replay, `tests/wsmedia.rs` and `tests/wsmedia_ipc.rs` use real sockets, processes and sleeps with generous windows; `wsmedia_ipc` takes about 7 s, `golden_wsmedia` about 1.5 s. The 5 s idle close of a hub leg is exercised with short idle values (40 to 150 ms) in the tests and with the real 5 s only by the smoke script.
* **The talkback join (Protect ring) is driven through `WsMediaHub` directly**, not through the hub's `configure`, because the hub builds the driver itself and the test needs to address its fake console over plain http and its fake `ffmpeg`. The `join` closure is the same function the hub passes. The `ConnectAnswerer` branch for `unifi_protect_talk` (an in-process ring, not a call out) is covered by `tests/talk_ipc.rs` only as far as the join being attempted (the answer fails against the unreachable host there).
* Both fakes of the golden (Go's recorders, Rust's) are written separately from the same description; a defect common to both would not show.
* RSS/CPU of a long call with an away phone: not measured.
